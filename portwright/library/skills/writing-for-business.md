# Writing for business readers

- Lead with the answer. The first sentence says what matters most, with its number.
- One idea per sentence. Short words. Active voice ("Sales rose 4%", not "An increase was observed").
- Numbers: round to what matters (4%, not 4.137%), always with units and the period ("in October").
- Compare only with what the data contains. No "as expected" or "strong performance" without a target.
- Bullets for three or more parallel facts; otherwise sentences.
- No jargon, internal codes or column names; use the business term ("headcount", not `emp_cnt`).
- Neutral tone. No exclamation marks, no emojis, no apologies.
- Never include passwords, personal data or information the reader would not see in the report itself.
